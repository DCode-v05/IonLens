The Commercialization Assessment, in plain terms

An outside analyst assessed your technology — the physics-constrained Bayesian framework for ion energy flux — the way an investor or incubator would. This page explains what the 63-page report actually says: the verdict, the numbers, the risks, and the recommended path, without the density.

00What this document is (and how it is written)

It is an Executive Commercialization Assessment Report — a third party judging whether your work is worth incubating and investing in. It is not marketing and it is not from your team; it deliberately separates what you claim from what the analyst could verify.

The whole report runs on one rule: every number carries a tag saying where it came from. Worth knowing, because it explains the report's cautious tone.

The four tags

[C] a claim by your team, not independently checked.   [CALC] the analyst's own arithmetic (inputs shown).   [A] the analyst's own count or search result.   [S1] an external source with a live URL.

Sentences that begin "Assessment:" are the analyst's opinion; "Projection (scenario):" are forward-looking guesses. Almost every hard number about your results is tagged [C] — meaning it is real to you, but the analyst has not seen the code run.

The central caution the report repeats

Every one of your benchmark figures is synthetic, self-reported, and not independently verified. Nothing has yet been tested against real laboratory data. Reading a synthetic result as a lab result, or a projected number as an achieved one, is a misreading. Keep this in mind for every "3.99%" that follows.

02The problem being solved

Ion energy flux at a plasma–surface boundary sets how fast a surface heats and erodes — and in all three target industries it is causal for lifetime, yet cannot be measured directly without perturbing it. The report shows two measurement routes, both of which fail in a documented way:

  • Intrusive probes perturb what they measure. A Langmuir probe in low-pressure argon is expected to underestimate density by ~50% S1. The field found it necessary to publish three formal "Recommended Practice" papers for probe use in one 2017 journal issue — "fields do not codify practice that is working reliably."
  • Single optical diagnostics see only part of the state. They do not perturb, but each returns a partial observable, leaving the problem underdetermined.

That structure — partial non-perturbing observables plus a known physical model — is the textbook setup for an inverse problem, which is why the approach is appropriate in principle. What the gap costs today:

Electric propulsionNASA's NEXT thruster life test ran June 2005 to Sept 2013 (~8 yrs 3 mo), 50,170 hours, 902 kg of xenon — qualification measured in calendar years. HERMeS ran multiple multi-thousand-hour wear tests just as a pathfinder S3, S13, S14.

03The value proposition — and what it is NOT

Measured against the real baseline (a retarding-field analyser inserted into the plasma), the report accepts four selling points. The one it rates highest is the third:

  1. Designed to leave the plasma undisturbed (all four channels non-intrusive).
  2. Returns a calibrated posterior (an answer with honest error bars), not a single number.
  3. It states what it cannot resolve. "A framework that declares its own blind spots is more useful to a qualification engineer than one that returns a confident number everywhere — and it is the hardest claim for a competitor to retrofit."
  4. Reaches where hardware cannot go (optical access where probes do not survive).
Three claims the report says you must NOT make
  • Not "the first Bayesian fusion of plasma diagnostics." Integrated Data Analysis has run at ASDEX Upgrade since ~2004; the method is well published.
  • Not "more channels than anyone." Minerva at W7-X fuses the same four; Minerva at JET models ~ten. And your own benchmark showed a fourth channel added naively made things worse — so channel count was never the edge.
  • Not "faster." Neural-network surrogates for this exact problem already exist.
The claim that survives — memorise this sentence

"Inference of ion energy flux at a surface, through a sheath forward model, from non-intrusive optical channels only." The report verified that no competing framework it read occupies this exact position. That precise sentence — not "Bayesian" and not "four channels" — is your defensible ground.

04The technology, as the analyst understood it

The report files you under a category that does not yet exist on any analyst's list: model-based inferential instrumentation — software that turns an incomplete set of measurements into an estimate of an unmeasurable quantity, with a stated uncertainty, by inverting a first-principles model.

Architecture (your description, [C], not independently run):

  • Observation model y = F(x) + ε, with F a strict four-layer chain: plasma physics → emission/scattering → optical transport → detector.
  • Inversion via Bayes, with sheath theory as a structural prior — the sampler can only propose states that satisfy Poisson's equation. The report calls this "the technically distinctive decision": impossible states become unreachable, not merely improbable.
  • Six inference engines behind one interface, for cross-validation; four fused optical channels (OES, LIF, Thomson, interferometry).
The one number the report says must not be misread

Your "~33 hours on one RTX A4000" for the full simulation programme is a projection, not a runtime. It rests on an assumed GPU throughput that has not been measured. The L1 fluid solver was since measured (86 ms vs a 1 s estimate — good), but the L2 particle-push speed the 33 h actually depends on was not, and you carried it forward rather than closing it. The report says flagging this yourselves is "among the strongest credibility signals in the submission."

The T2 figure is a mean over 10 noise realisations against the true flux at a sealed point (an analytic 9.40 kW/m²). By your own rule you must quote the two supporting numbers beside it: density −3.54% and temperature +8.20%.

The commercial claim is calibration, not accuracy

The number that decides whether anyone buys this is coverage: 8 of 10 credible intervals cover the truth at the 90% level (against 0 of 10 for naive fusion). In plain terms: your error bars are roughly honest. Two caveats the report insists go with it: at only 10 runs, 8/10 and 9/10 are statistically indistinguishable; and 80% against a 90% target is mild under-coverage.

The finding worth more than the headline number

Fusing four channels naively gave 25.7% error at 0/10 coverage — confidently wrong, with error bars four times too narrow. A per-channel audit traced it to one channel (OES) understating its own uncertainty by 29–138×. Down-weighting that channel (rather than dropping it) recovered 3.99% at 8/10 coverage with all four channels kept. This is the story the report says to lead with: catching an inverse problem lying to itself, which is exactly what the technology is for.

The +8.2% temperature bias, and the biggest open gap

The temperature error was traced to a fixable modelling detail (a LIF sampling node that is not quite the sheath edge) — "a located, fixable defect rather than a diffuse one." But the highest-value missing item in the whole report is the identifiability map: the analysis of which regions of operating space can actually be resolved. Your machinery for it exists but it was not computed. This is the joint-highest risk and the one with the least evidence — and it needs only compute and time, not money or hardware.

06Readiness: TRL 2, and what that does and doesn't mean

The report maps you against NASA's official software readiness scale and assigns TRL 2: all four clauses met, including the two that require working code and experiments on synthetic data. "The gate this assessment named has been cleared."

Read TRL 2 precisely

TRL 2 is a statement about work performed, not work succeeding — the clause is met simply because the experiments were run. It also rests on your report of execution, not independent verification. TRL 3 needs validation against real laboratory data, which is the next real gate and is still open. An assessor's fair ask is to watch the benchmark actually run.

The maturity dashboard's most telling feature is its asymmetry: Documentation scores 4 while everything else scores 1–3. You have specified far more than you have built, and built far more than you have validated — "the correct shape to start from" for a rigorous researcher without lab access. It becomes a warning only if it persists after funding.

10IP and the moat

The patent search (18 patents reviewed, 23 queries) found no patent claiming exactly your combination: Bayesian inversion of multiple fused non-intrusive optical diagnostics against a physics-based sheath model to produce a calibrated posterior over ion energy flux. The closest live art is a Lam Research patent coupling an optical signal to a sheath quantity inside a control loop.

The finding that inverts the usual reading

A thin patent record here is a novelty risk, not open whitespace. The non-patent literature is dense with exactly this method — and dense non-patent prior art is a validity problem (it is the likeliest reason the patent record looks empty). Practical consequence: a literature-based prior-art search is worth more than more patent keyword searching. If you file, the defensible combination is the method plus a control action conditioned on a posterior credible interval — the piece the academic literature does not contain.

Filing cost is small (~$4,352 in government PCT fees before attorney costs), with possible fee reductions — so cost is not the barrier; defensibility is.

  • You reported the negative four-channel result against your own stated differentiator.

The bibliometrics carry one honest tension worth knowing before a judge raises it: the niche itself ("plasma sheath" + "ion energy") is thin and flat — which reads either as untouched whitespace or as a field the community found intractable. The defensible framing is the adjacent one: the method (uncertainty quantification in plasma) is the fastest-growing series measured, while the specific intersection is unoccupied. That is a real tailwind without over-claiming.
